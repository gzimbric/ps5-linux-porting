## First exact-artifact hardware result

Before flashing, the downloaded archive and expanded image matched the release
identities above. The complete image-sized device readback also matched raw
SHA-256 `25a0ef68…`. After the boot, all five files on the mounted FAT partition
matched their published sizes and SHA-256 values.

One cold launch with the 170,904-byte bounded Wi-Fi loader
(`e9fe6469943e97a635eccd3f674e55876b56cd1bbd69023eee3af4acea3a1b5f`)
then passed on a CFI-1115B running retail firmware 11.60:

- Linux 7.3.0 reached a directly observed HDMI picture and authenticated wired
  SSH. The root filesystem was mounted read/write and expanded to 230.6 GiB on
  the 248 GB test drive, and systemd reached a running state.
- The loader supplied the console's 498,108-byte IW620 firmware. The image's
  firmware service installed it, `moal` bound to the adapter and scans returned
  nearby networks. A join through Plasma then received DHCP, and two separate
  checks sent 3/3 and 2/2 successful pings explicitly through `wlp64s0f7`.
- The internal `1286:2059` Bluetooth function registered one powered controller.
  A DualSense was discovered, paired, bonded and trusted through the normal
  BlueZ/`bluetoothctl` path, automatically reconnected, registered through
  `hid-playstation`, exposed controller, motion and touchpad devices, and
  produced raw input events. It was still connected after the capture.
- Plasma → Gaming Mode passed. On that first launch the visible tty showed
  Steam downloading and installing a 503,789 KB client update before the
  SteamOS interface appeared. The temporary tty view cleared when the update
  finished; it was test timing during the normal first-launch path, not an
  image or display failure. Gaming Mode → Plasma then passed visually; KWin
  and plasmashell were present within about one second and no overlapping
  compositor remained. The returned desktop exposed a separate release bug:
  KDE reported **System policy prevents control of network connections**.
  Polkit evaluated the returned helper processes outside the active seat and
  required authentication.
- The startup performance service reached GPU `simple_ondemand`, CPU
  `schedutil` and CPU boost off. The released fan helper still left the
  autoservo target at 86 C. The documented per-boot hotfix changed the readback
  to 65 C for this session; the persistent correction on `main` is not part of
  this artifact.
- With the 65 C target active, a 20-second, 16-worker CPU load held all sampled
  CPU policies near 3.19 GHz. The measured temperature started at 38 C, peaked
  at 47 C and read 37 C three seconds after the load ended. That bounded check
  passed; it is not a thermal-equilibrium or long-duration stability result,
  and it did not measure fan RPM.
- The base image left `systemd-networkd-wait-online.service` enabled alongside
  NetworkManager. It timed out after two minutes and was the only failed unit,
  even though networking and SSH remained up. Disabling the unused service and
  resetting its failed state left NetworkManager active, SSH connected and no
  failed units. This cleanup is not in the published image.

A live `/etc/polkit-1/rules.d/49-ps5-networkmanager.rules` correction allowed
only nine explicitly listed NetworkManager actions for the `steam` account.
After installation, authorization checks from the actual `plasmashell` and `kded6` processes
returned yes, an unprivileged Wi-Fi rescan passed, and no new authorization
failure appeared. KWin and plasmashell remained the only active compositor and
desktop shell. That rule is not present in the published Image 3 identity and
must be applied as the tester guide describes; the current next-image source
includes it.

This closes the first exact-artifact boot, GUI Wi-Fi join, internal-Bluetooth
pairing/input and two-way session-handoff gates. Image 3 remains a prerelease:
two to four more cold boots, cold-boot Wi-Fi and Bluetooth reconnect, a
60-minute coexistence run, a longer thermal/stability run, performance opt-out,
storage integrity, video decode, Steam Offline Mode and broader hardware
coverage remain open.
